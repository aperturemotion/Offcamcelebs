OffCamCelebs 038 — "Every Dollar" (Ryan Reynolds / Mint Mobile, $1.35B) — CORRECTED VO SCRIPT (final, 2026-11-21)
Read this version, not the brief's. The two lines marked * were changed after checking every line at source.

------------------------------------------------------------------ PASTE-READY
Ryan Reynolds' fifteen-dollar-a-month phone company sold for one point three five billion dollars. Every headline said so. We waited for the last payment.

Reynolds bought into Mint Mobile in twenty nineteen and fronted its ads.

In twenty twenty-three, T-Mobile agreed to buy it. Up to one point three five billion.

On closing day: four hundred and twenty million in cash, plus five hundred and thirty-six million in shares.

The rest depended on Mint hitting targets. That's where numbers usually shrink.

Not this time. In twenty twenty-five, the targets were dropped. The rest became the maximum, minus what was already paid.

On October ninth, T-Mobile paid the final four hundred and twenty million.

The catch: Reynolds wasn't the only owner. Reports put his stake at about a quarter, roughly three hundred million.

To be fair, he never called it his billion. He joked T-Mobile beat a last-minute bid from his mom.

So, one point three five billion? Every dollar got paid. Our rating: legit.

Send us who to audit next.
------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY

* Line 1: "fifteen-dollar phone company" → "fifteen-dollar-a-month phone company".
  $15 is Mint's entry MONTHLY rate (its ads: "Premium Wireless for $15 per Month"; the 5GB plan), paid upfront in 3-, 6- or
  12-month blocks; the National Advertising Division found the $15 pricing "not clearly disclosed". "Fifteen-dollar phone
  company" reads as a $15 phone or a $15 total. "A month" makes it true and keeps the hook.

* Line 8: "Reports put his stake at about a quarter. Still roughly three hundred million." → "Reports put his stake at about a
  quarter, roughly three hundred million."
  The brief's guardrail: his ~25% and ~$300M are reports, always "reports put…". As two sentences, "Still roughly three
  hundred million" stood alone as our own statement. As one, "reports put" carries both figures. (Fortune, 17 Mar 2023:
  "Reynolds, who owns an estimated 25% stake in Mint Mobile, will receive about $300 million from the deal, the Wall Street
  Journal reported." Neither T-Mobile nor Mint ever disclosed his stake; Mint's 2019 release called the investment
  undisclosed.)

* Years are written as spoken ("twenty nineteen", "twenty twenty-three", "twenty twenty-five") for the voice; the screen shows
  digits.

KEPT, AND WHY (checked, true as written)
- "sold for one point three five billion" — the headline's own words (NPR, 15 Mar 2023: "T-Mobile buys Ryan Reynolds' Mint
  Mobile in a $1.35 billion deal"); the film then shows it was "up to", and that the maximum was paid. "Reynolds'" matches
  NPR's headline; he was a part-owner, and line 8 says so.
- "Reynolds bought into Mint Mobile in twenty nineteen and fronted its ads" — Mint Mobile release, 25 Nov 2019, "Ryan Reynolds
  Purchases Ownership Stake in Mint Mobile" (amount undisclosed); he starred in its ads, made by his agency Maximum Effort.
- "In twenty twenty-three, T-Mobile agreed to buy it. Up to one point three five billion." — T-Mobile 10-Q, Q1 2023: "On March
  9, 2023, we entered into a Merger and Unit Purchase Agreement … for a maximum purchase price of $1.35 billion"; announced 15
  Mar 2023 ("T-Mobile will pay up to a maximum of $1.35 billion in a combination of 39% cash and 61% stock to acquire Ka'ena").
- "On closing day: four hundred and twenty million in cash, plus five hundred and thirty-six million in shares." — T-Mobile
  10-Q, Q2 2024 (filed 31 Jul 2024): "on May 1, 2024 … we completed the Ka'ena Acquisition"; "we transferred $420 million in
  cash and 3,264,952 shares of T-Mobile common stock valued at $536 million … for a total payment fair value of $956 million."
- "The rest depended on Mint hitting targets." — the same filing: "up to an additional $403 million … dependent upon Ka'ena's
  achievement of specified performance indicators", payable 1 Aug 2026. True at closing; the next lines say it changed.
- "In twenty twenty-five, the targets were dropped. The rest became the maximum, minus what was already paid." — T-Mobile 10-Q,
  Q2 2025 (filed 23 Jul 2025): "On June 30, 2025, we amended the Merger and Unit Purchase Agreement to set the calculation of
  the earnout as the difference between the maximum purchase price of $1.35 billion and the upfront payment, as adjusted, and
  removed the requirement for Ka'ena to achieve specified performance indicators." ("As adjusted": $17M of the upfront was
  returned in Q4 2024, raising the earnout by the same amount.)
- "On October ninth, T-Mobile paid the final four hundred and twenty million." — T-Mobile 10-Q, Q3 2026 (filed 28 Oct 2026):
  "on October 9, 2026, we transferred $164 million in cash and 1,510,371 shares of T-Mobile common stock in satisfaction of our
  $420 million earnout obligation."
- "He joked T-Mobile beat a last-minute bid from his mom." — his statement in T-Mobile's release, 15 Mar 2023: "… beat out an
  aggressive last-minute bid from my mom Tammy Reynolds …" (exact opening words seen in excerpts only).
- "He never called it his billion" — no statement found in which he claims $1.35B as his own (absence of evidence; fair).
- "Every dollar got paid" — the contract's maximum: the amended earnout is by definition $1.35B minus the adjusted upfront, and
  it was paid in full. NEVER shown as $956M + $420M (= $1,376M: different share prices on different dates).
