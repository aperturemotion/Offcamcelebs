037 "Afterpay: $29B or $13.8B?" — VO SCRIPT (corrected 2026-11-18; read THIS, not the brief)

Afterpay sold for twenty-nine billion dollars. That's the number in the headlines.
The buyer's own filing says thirteen point eight.
Let me explain.
In August twenty twenty-one, Jack Dorsey's Square agreed to buy Afterpay.
But it didn't pay in cash. It paid in its own shares.
Every Afterpay share would become point three seven five Square shares.
At the time, one Square share was worth two hundred and forty-seven dollars. That's where twenty-nine billion came from.
And here's the catch.
The deal took six months to close. By then, Square had renamed itself Block, and its share price had roughly halved.
So when Block booked the deal with the S.E.C., the shares it handed over were worth thirteen point eight billion.
To be fair, twenty-nine billion was the real value on announcement day. The deal fixed the number of shares, not the dollars, and Afterpay's owners got those shares.
So twenty-nine billion was the price on day one. Not the price paid.
Our rating: inflated.
