Tiger Woods turned down eight hundred million dollars to join LIV Golf. Every headline said so. We went looking for the offer.

Tiger is golf's biggest name, with fifteen major championships.

In twenty twenty-two, LIV, a new Saudi-backed tour, went after golf's stars with huge contracts.

The number came from LIV's boss, Greg Norman. In June, he told The Washington Post the offer was mind-blowingly enormous. High nine digits.

In August, on Fox News, the host listed seven hundred million, then eight hundred. Norman answered: somewhere in that neighborhood. He said the offer was made before he was even in charge.

Then the head of Saudi golf told The New Yorker: I never offered him that money. Not even close.

And Tiger? He has never confirmed any number. No contract. No document.

To be fair, Tiger never joined LIV. He stayed with the PGA Tour.

So, eight hundred million dollars? Our rating: unsupported.

LINE BY LINE (sources)
- "turned down eight hundred million … Every headline said so": NPR, CBS News, ESPN, NBC, Fortune, 2 Aug 2022 ("Tiger Woods
  turned down $700–800 million…"); the figure is never Woods' own (hence "every headline said").
- "fifteen major championships": Masters 1997, 2001, 2002, 2005, 2019; PGA 1999, 2000, 2006, 2007; US Open 2000, 2002, 2008;
  Open 2000, 2005, 2006.
- "In twenty twenty-two, LIV, a new Saudi-backed tour, went after golf's stars": LIV Golf, funded by Saudi Arabia's Public
  Investment Fund, launched June 2022 and signed players on guaranteed contracts (reported; no figures used).
- "In June, he told The Washington Post … mind-blowingly enormous. High nine digits": Norman to the Washington Post, reported
  6 Jun 2022 (Golf Digest; CBS Sports): "Mind-blowingly enormous … We're talking about high nine digits."
- "In August, on Fox News, the host listed seven hundred million, then eight hundred. Norman answered: somewhere in that
  neighborhood. He said the offer was made before he was even in charge": Tucker Carlson Tonight, aired 1 Aug 2022 (NPR, AP,
  2 Aug 2022): Carlson offered $700M, $800M, $1B; Norman: "They'd originally approached Tiger before I became CEO … the
  number is somewhere in that neighborhood."
- "the head of Saudi golf told The New Yorker: I never offered him that money. Not even close": Majed Al Sorour, CEO of Golf
  Saudi, to The New Yorker, 2022 (Golf Monthly; Golf.com: "I never offered him that money, not even close to that"); he
  described the package as including equity and a cut of sponsorships.
- "He has never confirmed any number": no on-record statement from Woods on the amount (all coverage, 2022–2026).
- "To be fair, Tiger never joined LIV. He stayed with the PGA Tour": he never played a LIV event; he publicly backed the PGA
  Tour (2022 Open press conference) and later joined its policy board.
- Verdict: UNSUPPORTED — the $800M rests on Norman agreeing with a TV host's figure; no document; the Saudi side disputes it.
