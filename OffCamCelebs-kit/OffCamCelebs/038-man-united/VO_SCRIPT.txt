038 "Bought for £790M. United paid the bill." — VO SCRIPT (corrected 2026-11-20; read THIS, not the brief)

The Glazers bought Manchester United for seven hundred and ninety million pounds.
More than five hundred million was borrowed. And within a year, most of that debt was on United's own books.
Let me explain.
The American family took over in two thousand and five, mostly with loans.
Nearly five years later, United itself sold five hundred million pounds of bonds to pay off the bank loans.
So ticket, shirt and TV money went on paying for the takeover.
ESPN says that by twenty eighteen, the cost had passed one billion pounds.
And here's the worst part.
Twenty-one years later, United's borrowings stand at about six hundred and ninety million.
Last season, net finance costs alone were sixty-nine point six million. About one hundred and ninety thousand a day.
To be fair, under the Glazers, United won five league titles and the Champions League.
But seven hundred and ninety million was the price. United has been paying the bill ever since.
Our rating: inflated.
Send us who to audit next.

CHANGES FROM THE BRIEF (5)
1. "And most of that debt landed on United's own books." -> "And within a year, most of that debt was on United's own books."
   In 2005 only the £265m loan was secured on the club; the £275m of securities had "no hold over Manchester United's
   assets" (Irish Examiner, 13 May 2005), so in 2005 it was not "most". The August 2006 refinancing put about £525m of
   bank debt on the club, with about £135–138m of PIK debt outside it (Irish Times; Irish Examiner).
2. "Five years later" -> "Nearly five years later": the bonds were issued in January 2010, 4 years 8 months after May 2005.
3. "By twenty eighteen, ESPN put the cost at over one billion pounds." -> "ESPN says that by twenty eighteen, the cost had
   passed one billion pounds." ESPN's own pieces date the £1bn mark to 2018 (Ogden) and to 2015 (a later piece); both make
   "by 2018" true, and the line no longer says ESPN wrote it in 2018. Always labelled as ESPN's; it includes dividends.
4. "United still owes about six hundred and ninety million" -> "United's borrowings stand at about six hundred and ninety
   million": the guardrail (today's £689m is current borrowing, refinanced several times, not the 2005 loan).
5. "finance costs" -> "net finance costs": the release's own label; it includes a £10.0m unrealised FX loss.
