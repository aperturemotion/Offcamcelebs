## THE RULES THAT DECIDE WHETHER A FILM LOOKS LIKE THEIRS (R1–R40)

### The world
- R1 A DARK-GREY STUDIO, NOT BLACK. Base tone about #201f21, neutral (not blue). Only 18–40% of a frame is near-black; 34–50% is dark grey.
- R2 Every frame is lit top to bottom: a lit floor, a visible horizon at 35–65% of the height, and a backdrop with a soft sweep of light behind the subject. The subject is never on flat black (cards excepted).
- R3 Highlights are rationed: 2.5–4.5% of the frame is bright; no white hot spots. Vignette gentle (centre ÷ corners 1.7–3.3). No grain.
- R4 The floor is rough: a blurred pool under a light, never a readable mirror image.
- R5 One or two LIGHT scenes per film (a pale grey table seen from above, a pale map, a blueprint), entered by a hard cut, usually right after a card. Paper appears only there.
- R6 One palette per film; one accent colour per beat. Colour covers ~2% of the frame.

### Objects
- R7 (★ read with P16: real, recognisable objects with real logos, never grey boxes) One chunky, literal, toy-like thing per sentence, from the story's world: machines, vehicles, containers, a safe, a scale, a shop front, a card terminal, price tags, coin stacks, a calendar, a gavel. 16 object scenes in 85 s.
- R8 NO documents to read: no newspapers, contracts, certificates, filings, receipts as subjects. In-scene text is at most three words or one figure with a unit.
- R9 NO people and no photographs: a person is a glossy pawn, or a nameplate with a microphone; a country is a flag ball.
- R10 Bodies are matte black with broad soft gradients and thin specular lines on bevelled edges (3–10 px). NO glowing outlines, no white edge strips.
- R11 Objects ENTER BY ACTING: roll in, drop with blur and a bounce, swing in, grow, flip, pop up, print out, pour. Nothing fades or slides in politely. Then they act the sentence.
- R12 Something happens every 1–1.5 s inside every shot. A shot where only the camera moves is a fault.
- R13 Objects recur with one change (the locomotive three times, the drums three times). Size varies on purpose: full width, medium, or tiny-in-space.
- R14 Gold is khaki-gold in the body (#8a6f47) with pale cream highlights; it means money itself.

### Figures and charts
- R15 Every figure is ON an object (a lit plate, a tag, a screen, a sign) or IS one (solid numerals standing on the floor, depth 30–40% of their height). Never a floating label.
- R16 Figures COUNT UP to their value in 0.6–1.6 s and land on the spoken word; the landing is marked by light (a thin ring spreading on the floor, a bloom).
- R17 Type is heavy and wide; cap height 5–10% of the frame; a hero figure spans 50–94% of the width.
- R18 Warm white (#f8f2f2) by default; pale yellow (#f6f2bd) for money; RED only for the bad number (it turns red as it lands); green only for "pass". A brand colour only on the brand's own thing.
- R19 Compared figures share a frame. Charts are things: a ramp that climbs, a ring that fills, columns that grow, a dial.
- R20 Only 4–6 rounded figures per film.

### Camera and cutting
- R21 The picture moves in 95% of frames; the default is a slow push-in (×1.1–×3) with a small rise, tilt or roll. Holds still drift.
- R22 Big moves follow the sentence: ride along a vehicle, orbit to a container's doors, crane up a ramp. Low for vehicles and tall things, high and slanted for tables and maps.
- R23 Neighbouring objects share one set and the camera travels between them; a NEW idea or ground is a HARD CUT. 9–16 cuts per film plus a few whip pans of 6–15 blurred frames. NEVER a travel through empty dark, a dissolve, or black frames.
- R24 Shots run 1.7–11 s, median ~4.5 s; one move often carries two or three beats.
- R25 Framing: the lit picture is centred 44–51% down the frame and spans 80–94% of its width; the named object is 40–95% of the width and is often cropped.
- R26 Strong depth of field (the subject sharp, near and far soft) and motion blur on fast moves and fast acting.

### Cards and captions
- R27 Turn cards: black, words only: a small warm-white lead-in landing word by word, then one big bold-italic coloured word (55–80% of the width) and a radial glow blooming behind it. No floor, no reflection, no camera move. 1.3–2.9 s, hard cuts in and out. Amber "explain", pale gold "twist", red "worst part"/"catch".
- R28 Captions: one phrase of 1–4 words, colour #e9e2e1, regular, about 28–33 px at 1080, its top about 14% down (y ≈ 266–282), a 1–2 frame swap, never moves, drawn over the picture.

### Script
- R29 The hook is three sentences in 10–15 s: the setup, the number contrast, the stake. The first figure is said and on screen within 1.5–5 s.
- R30 About 155 words a minute; short plain sentences; second person; numbers rounded.
- R31 Frame 0 is mid-action. The ending replays the opening shot with one thing changed and a short punchline, and stops 0.6–1.8 s after the last word.
- R32 They show no CHANNEL logo bug, verdict card, sources or call to action (brand logos are everywhere in their scenes: see P16 in the preferences). (The Payouts keeps a SPOKEN rating and sign-off: Timur's brief.)

## WHERE THESE CORRECT THE RULES WRITTEN EARLIER (P1–P12 in the preferences)
- P1 said "a black studio": it is a lit DARK-GREY studio with a backdrop (R1–R2).
- P5 said captions sit 10% down: they sit ~14–15% down, ~28–33 px (R28).
- P7 said places are joined by moving through the dark: they are joined by HARD CUTS and whips; travel happens only inside one set (R23).
- P6/P11 made the turn cards extruded 3-D letters on a floor: theirs are flat words with a glow (R27). Ours are rendered in the 3-D scene but must LOOK like theirs: flat-faced, no floor, no reflection.
- The first 037 cut's mirror floor, glowing white outlines, mint figures everywhere, paper subjects and framed photo all break R4, R10, R18, R8, R9.
