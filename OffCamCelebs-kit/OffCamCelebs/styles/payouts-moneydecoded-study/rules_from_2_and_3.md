# RULES read from references v2 (Afterpay, 84.9 s) and v3 (passport, 67.1 s)

Every rule names where it was seen (v2 #n / v3 #n = shot number in study_v2.md / study_v3.md). Pixel figures are at 720×1280; ×1.5 for 1080×1920.

## A. The frame and its darkness
1. The film is DARK: mean frame luminance 44.6/255 (v2) and 34.3/255 (v3); 41% and 54% of all pixels are darker than 16/255. The void is #030303, never pure #000 and never tinted except a faint red-black in the far top corners (v2 #2, #8, #9).
2. The lit subject is still BIG: lit content below the caption spans a median 87% (v2) / 94% (v3) of the frame WIDTH and 64% / 58% of its HEIGHT, centred 59% down the frame. The subject sits in the middle-to-lower-middle, not the top.
3. The lower 25–40% of most dark shots is floor: a dark, slightly glossy ground that fades to black, carrying a soft grey light pool and BLURRED reflections (never a sharp mirror image; you cannot read a reflected word) (v2 #2, #9, #10; v3 #3).
4. Each film has LIGHT-ground scenes cut in hard: v2 three (phone 7.6 s, card terminal's white table 28.4 s, receipt 65.2 s), v3 one (open passport, 36 s). A light scene is a mid/light grey seamless table seen from above (#494a51 → #2b2c31 in v2 #3; whiter under the receipt and terminal). They are where documents and screens are read.
5. No frame is ever empty or near-black except the 2–4 frames of a whip and the first ~0.25 s of a turn card before its first word.

## B. Objects
6. Every sentence gets ONE literal object (or one small set) that IS the thing said: jacket, price tags, shop tablet, card terminal, bars of gold, scale, shop front, calendar, donut gauge, booklet+receipt, shelf, queue, coin stack (v2); passport on a plinth, scale, e-gate, departures board, vending machine, stamp, hearing desk, runway sign (v3). 16 object scenes in 85 s; 8 in 67 s.
7. Objects are everyday THINGS, not paperwork. Paper appears once per film, on a light ground, filling the frame (v2 #15 receipt; v3 #8 visa pages). No newspapers, contracts, certificates, filings or framed photos anywhere.
8. Money as an amount is shown as gold: stacked gold slabs (v2 #9), gold coins pouring into a box (v3 #7), a coin stack (v2 #18), coins dropping beside a nameplate (v3 #10). Small weights carry "$50"/"$200" (v2 #10).
9. People are NEVER shown. A person is an abstract glossy figure of two spheres with a ring collar and dot eyes (v2 #11 shopper, #17 queue: white = you, black = someone else), or a NAMEPLATE plus a microphone whose ring lights red when they speak (v3 #10). Companies are their word mark on an object (lozenge sign, tag, flag screen).
10. Bodies are matte black/graphite with broad soft highlights and a thin bright edge only where a real edge catches light (rails, chrome stands, tablet bezel). They are NOT outlined: no continuous glowing line round every edge.
11. Chrome is used for thin structures (scale, stands, stanchions, rails): reads as bright lines. Glass is used for cases and gate flaps (v3 #1, #3, #4).
12. Objects are chunky and slightly toy-like: big radii, thick slabs, simplified detail (terminal keys as 12 grey blocks, products as plain cylinders and boxes).
13. An object is big in frame: hero objects reach 45–95% of the width at the end of their push-in (tablet 95%, scale 85–100%, disc 75%, vending machine 85%, phone 45%, terminal 55%). They are routinely CROPPED by the frame edges.
14. One world per run of shots: adjacent ideas share furniture so the camera can travel between them (the jacket and the four tags hang on the same rail, v2 #5–6; two shop fronts side by side, v2 #11; two speakers on one desk, v3 #10; the gate and the board above it, v3 #5; a field of plinths, v3 #1).

## C. Figures and text in the scene
15. A figure is always ON or BESIDE its object: on a plinth's face ("$422"), on a screen, on a tag, on a price plate, on a sign board, or as solid extruded numerals standing on the floor in front of the object (v2 #2, #9, #18).
16. Figures COUNT UP to their value over 0.25–1.2 s, digits rolling ($10.8B→$39B in 1.0 s; 29c→80c in 0.6 s; $136→$206; $5→$422 in 1.2 s; $133M→$822M over 3.2 s while its wedge sweeps). No figure simply appears, except small list items.
17. The landing of a key figure is marked by LIGHT: a thin ring expanding across the floor from the object plus a bloom (v2 2.5 s, 10.5 s, 36.5 s, 41.5 s, 83.25 s).
18. Figure colour: white (#f7f5f6 / #e1dde2) by default; warm cream-gold (#f3f29d, #f5e393, #e8aa50) when it is money earned/charged; red only for the bad thing (LATE, NOT ALLOWED, TAX, AUSTRALIA's row, the cross); green only for "pass" (tick, Singapore's row). Brand mint (#bdd9d4) only on the brand's own lozenge.
19. Figure size: floor numerals 78% of frame width with a 32 px cap height (2.5% of frame height) (v2 #2, #18); a plinth figure ~30–40% of width and 6–7% of height when close (v3 #1); a screen figure ~20–25% of width (80c, $8.00).
20. Type: heavy WIDE grotesque capitals and numerals (Archivo Black / Expanded look) for figures, signs and nameplates; small tracked capitals for labels (INTEREST, PAID TO SHOP, LATE FEES); a typewriter mono only on receipts, stamps and the LCD board.
21. Text is emissive print or solid letters with a soft bloom; never a floating 2-D label. At most 2–3 short text items are readable at once, except a list object (board, shelf plates, receipt).
22. A figure stays readable 1.1–3 s after it lands before the camera leaves it ($8.00 2.25 s; 80c 1.3 s; $822M 1.1 s; end numerals 1.6 s).
23. Two figures that are compared are in the SAME frame at the same time: $192.00 / $8.00; 80c / $8; $422 / $206; $422 / $50; $87M / $822M.

## D. Action
24. Something HAPPENS about every 1–1.5 s inside every shot: a drop, a flip, a count, a stamp, a slide, a light change. No shot is a still object with a moving camera.
25. Things arrive by FALLING or SWINGING in with motion blur and settle (jacket swings in; weights, bags, slabs, coins, a glass case and a passport drop; tags flip; a stamp slams). Nothing fades in.
26. Stamps and solid words SLAM onto a surface at an angle and stay (LATE, NOT ALLOWED, +15%, JAN 2025–27).
27. State changes are flips: a tag turns to show NOW / WEEK 2; a sign cell turns PASSPORT → TAX; a screen changes APPROVED → BANK FEE.
28. Repetition is acted out one by one on the words: four $50 tags, four weights, four ticks on the calendar, three stamps.
29. Lights act: a screen flashes on a card tap; a mic ring turns red when someone speaks; gate edges turn green; a shop window lights up; a blue wedge brightens when full.

## E. Camera
30. The camera NEVER stops. Every shot is one continuous move, almost always a PUSH-IN (×1.1 to ×3 over the shot) combined with a small rise, tilt or roll.
31. Angles: object shots are three-quarter or frontal at about the object's own height or slightly above; table shots (light ground) are high, 50–70° down; floor-figure shots are low. Wide-feeling lens with strong perspective on close objects.
32. Between neighbouring objects the camera TRAVELS in the same world (truck along the rail, pan to the other pan of the scale, truck to the next shop, rise from the gate to the board, pull back from one plinth to the field). 9 of 17 transitions in v2 are travels/whips.
33. A whip is 2–4 frames of heavy blur (v2 1.0 s, 22.2 s, 54.0 s; v3 52.0 s).
34. Hard cuts are used when the ground changes (dark ↔ light) or the idea jumps: 5 in v2, 3 in v3, plus the cuts into and out of turn cards.
35. Shot length: v2 mean 4.7 s (1.7–9.0); v3 mean 6.1 s (1.6–10.4). Long shots are long because the object keeps acting (donut 9 s, plinth 10.4 s, desk 10.5 s).
36. The camera follows the action inside a shot (down the calendar column, down the receipt as it prints, along the pages to each new stamp, to the BUDGET box as coins pour).

## F. Light
37. One soft key from above per object, making a grey pool on the floor round it; a faint rim. Backgrounds fall to black within about one object-width.
38. Emissive things bloom softly (screens, lit signs, gold). The brightest element of a dark shot is its figure or sign.
39. Coloured light is rare and local: gold bloom round gold; a red glow under the red-lit mic; green gate edges.
40. No lens flares, no visible light cones or shafts, no neon tube outlines, no grid, no particles except falling coins.

## G. Captions
41. One line, centred (x = 360), text box y 178–204: centre 14.9% down the frame, ~24 px font (3.4% of frame width; ≈ 37 px at 1080 wide, centre y ≈ 287 at 1920 high).
42. White, regular weight grotesque, sentence case with punctuation, no box, no outline, no shadow, no colour words.
43. 1–4 words per chunk (median width 200 px = 28% of the frame), swapping on the spoken word; the new chunk shows dimmed for ~1 frame then full white.
44. The caption is drawn over whatever passes behind it (calendar grid, tablet top, tag); the scene is not kept clear of it.
45. No caption during a turn card.

## H. Turn cards
46. 2 (v2) or 3 (v3) per film, 1.6–2.75 s each, at: the question after the hook (12–14 s / 26 s), an emphasis ("And it's not even close." 10.5 s), the twist or worst part (63.5 s / 45 s).
47. A card is FLAT TYPE on black: a white regular lead-in (1–2 lines, wide tracking, ~28 px cap) and ONE big bold-italic condensed word in colour (58% of frame width in v2 "money?", 42% "close.", 67% "expensive?"; ~60–70 px tall). Left-aligned block near the middle; in one variant the big word sits on the lead-in's line with "part." below (v3 #9).
48. Words land one at a time on their spoken onsets; each appears as a tiny bright smear for 1–2 frames and resolves.
49. A soft radial glow in the word's colour blooms behind the block when the big word lands (covering ~58% of frame height, full width, corners black) and holds 0.75–1 s. Colour: gold for a question/twist, orange for emphasis and "why", red for the worst part.
50. No object, floor, reflection or extruded letters on a card.

## I. Structure and script
51. The number is said in the first sentence (1.4 s / 1.7 s) and is on screen by 1.5 s, at full value by 2.5 s.
52. Hook = headline number + a superlative, then a contradiction ("And yet…", "So you'd think… It doesn't."), then the question as a card.
53. The body explains with small concrete numbers and one object per sentence, in cause order; comparisons are staged as two things side by side.
54. The last sentence restates the hook with the answer inside it, and the last SHOT returns the opening object and figure (v2: phone + $39,000,000,000 numerals; v3: $422 on the sign).
55. No logo, handle, sign-off, verdict label, progress bar or end card. The film ends 1.0–1.8 s after the last word on the held last image.
56. ~155 words a minute; sentences 2–26 words, median 11–12; second person; "about", "almost", "only" size the numbers.
57. Named real people appear only as nameplates; quoted exchanges are attributed in the voice.

## J. What they NEVER do
58. Never a flat chart, never a floating label, never a lower third or badge.
59. Never a photo of a person or a framed picture.
60. Never neon edge outlines on every object, never mirror-sharp reflections of text.
61. Never a static hold: no shot without a push or travel, no object without an action.
62. Never more than one accent colour family in a shot (gold, or red+blue in the donut, or green/red at the gate).
63. Never small objects in a big empty frame for more than the first second of a push-in.
64. Never a document as the hero in a dark scene.
