# RULES drawn from v1 (inland rail) and v4 (Norway's gas fund)

Every rule was seen in the frames and, where a number is given, measured (720×1280, 30 fps). "v1 s2" = v1, shot 2 of
`study_v1.md`. Percentages are of the frame unless said otherwise.

## A. The picture as a whole
1. The frame is one rendered 3-D scene: a floor plane with a visible horizon, a backdrop behind it, objects standing on the
   floor. There is no flat composited layer except the caption (every shot).
2. Two palettes exist, one per film, never mixed inside a film:
   - v1 "grey studio": neutral dark grey. Floor #1D1D1D–#1F1F1F, backdrop near-black #050407 with a soft grey spot behind
     the subject. Mean frame luminance 46/255; only 33% of pixels under 16/255.
   - v4 "black and gold": glossy black floor, black backdrop lit by COLOURED glows (gold, green, red). Mean luminance
     28/255; 56% of pixels under 16/255.
3. The backdrop always carries a soft light behind the subject (a grey haze in v1, a coloured glow in v4). The subject is
   never against pure flat black except on turn cards (v1 s2, s8, s12; v4 s1, s4, s10).
4. The floor meets the backdrop in a soft horizon line at 35–55% of the frame height in most shots; the lower third to half
   of the frame is floor (dark in v1, a blurred mirror in v4).
5. Reflections: v4's floor mirrors every object with a vertically blurred reflection; v1's floor is matte except under the
   price tag (v1 s8). Reflections are blurred and dimmer than the object, never a crisp mirror image.
6. Once or twice per film a LIGHT or strongly coloured scene breaks the dark: the pale map (v1 s6, s13; luminance 77–80),
   the blue blueprint (v1 s11, 88% of the frame lit), the white LNG plant (v4 s12). They come right after a black card.
7. No grain, no vignette overlay, no lens flare, no chromatic effects are visible. Depth of field is used rarely: the tag's
   back as it swings toward the lens (v1 s8).
8. Motion blur is strong and real on anything fast: objects dropping in, whip pans, coins in flight, the eraser
   (v1 s6, s11, s12; v4 s2, s6).

## B. Objects
9. Every subject is a literal, physical thing from the story's world: a locomotive, a wagon, a buffer stop, a road sign, a
   truck, a price tag, a bulldozer, a blueprint with a pen and an eraser, a silo and a radio dish, a shipping container, a
   ticket machine (v1); oil drums, coin stacks, a safe, LNG tanks, tanker ships, a coin (v4).
10. Objects are chunky, simplified, toy-like models: clean boxes and cylinders with bevelled edges, a few signature
    details (handrails, a bull bar, bogies, a spoked safe handle), no surface texture, no dirt.
11. Bodies are matte black or dark graphite and are read by pale rim highlights on their edges and by one bright detail
    (headlights, chrome, a dish rim). There is NO outline glow round a dark object.
12. Exceptions to dark bodies are deliberate and few: cream paper (the tag #E1D8CD, tickets), pale stone (map slab #93949D,
    rocks), white tanks (v4 s12), gold (coins), steel (the safe door).
13. Countries are flag-painted glossy BALLS; people are glossy black PAWNS (a sphere on a dome); companies are a small drum
    or a plant. No human figures, faces or hands appear anywhere, even where a pen draws or an eraser rubs (v1 s11).
14. One subject per shot. A second object appears only as the thing being compared (two drums, two lines) or as the place's
    landmark pair (silo + dish).
15. Objects ENTER by acting, never by fading in: roll in on rails (v1 s1, s4), drop from above with blur and a bounce (signs
    v1 s6; balls v4 s1; track segments v1 s12), swing in on a hook (v1 s8), grow (coin stack v4 s2), flip in (ring v4 s5),
    pop up from the floor (pawns v4 s4; plant pieces v4 s12), print out (tickets v1 s17).
16. Objects then DO the sentence: a wagon's price counts and turns red; container doors open on cash and shut; a pen draws
    a route and an eraser removes it; a coin bursts into 100 and 3 fall; a ship sails away with the money.
17. The same object returns later with a change, so the film has recurring characters (the locomotive: s1, s4, s7; the map:
    s6, s13; the truck: s7, s18; the drums: v4 s1, s2, s13; the coin stack: v4 s2, s4, s13).

## C. Size and composition
18. The subject is centred horizontally and sits in the middle band, y 30–65% of the frame; the caption owns the top 17%.
19. Size varies on purpose from shot to shot: full-width (the wagon plaque 100% → 66% text width; the safe 85%; the silo and
    dish 95%; the blueprint 100%), medium (tag 40–50% wide, 37–45% tall; gauge 51%; bulldozer 45%), and small-in-space for
    loneliness (the buffer stop and road sign: the sign is 8% of the width, lit area 5.5%, v1 s3).
20. Lit area (pixels brighter than 38/255 below the caption band) runs from 5% to 88% of the frame, median about 27% in v1
    and 21% in v4. Emptiness is dark floor and backdrop, never a hole in the scene.
21. Cropping by the frame is normal for big things (the locomotive at frame 0, the wagon, the truck's trailers, the map).
22. Three-quarter views for vehicles and machines; square-on views for plaques, signs, gauges and the final gantry.
23. Vertical format is used: tall things (coin stack 51–60% of the height, the tag, the ticket strip) and one-point depth
    down the frame (the track in v1 s19).

## D. Text and figures in the scene
24. Every figure is ON an object or IS an object: a plaque on a wagon, loco or container; a price tag; a bulldozer blade; a
    road sign; a printed ticket; a toll plate; a gauge; standing extruded numerals. Never a floating flat label.
25. Two kinds only: (a) emissive flat print on a dark plate, with a soft bloom ("$4.7 BILLION", "TAXPAYERS", "$14.5
    BILLION"); (b) solid extruded letters, white or gold or red, standing in the scene ("2018", the years, "$2", "3¢",
    "$0", "QUEENSLAND").
26. In-scene text is at most three words or one figure with a unit: "$4.7 BILLION", "PAID FOR BY / TAXPAYERS", "2010 PRICE /
    $4.7 / BILLION", "$500 / PER AUSTRALIAN", "TOLL | $14.5 BILLION", "78% / TAX", "$2 / TRILLION / FUND", "3¢ / IN EVERY $1",
    "$0 / TAX PAID". There is no sentence, paragraph, table or small print anywhere.
27. Type: a heavy, wide grotesque in capitals for words and figures; small spaced capitals for a kicker line ("PAID FOR
    BY", "2010 PRICE", "A YEAR", "TAX").
28. Size: a main figure's cap height is 3.4–7.4% of the frame height and its width 50–66% of the frame width when it is the
    subject ("$4.7 BILLION" 478 × 64 px; "TAXPAYERS" 366 × 44 px; the tag's "$4.7" 95 px tall; "$0" 84 px tall).
29. Figures APPEAR on their spoken word, within about 0.2 s: a dark plate lights up (dim brown → yellow over ~20 frames),
    numerals land one word at a time, a ticket prints.
30. Big figures COUNT up to their value over 0.7–1.5 s in 4–6 visible steps ($4.7 → 9.2 → 18.1 → 27.1 → 36.0 → 40.5 → $45;
    31 → 63 → 75 → 78%; $0B → $60B; $0.0T → $2.4T), then hold.
31. Colour of a figure carries its meaning: warm yellow #F6E996 or gold = money stated; RED #F4665B = the bad number (it
    turns red as it lands: "$45 BILLION", "$0"); white = neutral labels and dates; mint #79F1C4 = the growing line (v4).
32. A figure stays readable 0.8–4.3 s (median about 2.2 s), and the first one is on screen at 5.0–5.25 s, on its word.
33. Real-world signage keeps its real colours: green road signs with white type and a white border, a blue toll plate with
    a yellow border (v1 s3, s6, s19).

## E. Charts and maps
34. A chart is built as a thing: a ramp of track on stilts over a timeline of standing year numerals (v1 s12); a metal ring
    whose yellow arc fills (v4 s5); a 10 × 10 grid of coins from a burst dollar (v4 s10); a glowing line with a flag ball
    riding its tip (v4 s8).
35. A map is a slab lying on the ground seen at a slant (v1 s6) or a field of dots seen from above (v4 s9), with signs on
    pins, a route that draws itself, ships that sail.
36. Comparison is shown as two heights, two lines or two matching objects side by side, the same on both sides except for
    the one difference (v4 s2, s8, s13).
37. A share of a whole is shown by counting real pieces (3 coins of 100; 78% of a ring), not by a pie.

## F. Camera
38. The picture is in motion in 93–96% of frames (still in 3.6% of v1, 7.4% of v4); the longest still is 0.4–0.5 s and is
    used once, on the key figure (v1 s3; v4 s12).
39. The default is a slow push-in or a slow sideways dolly during every hold; the object grows 10–40% across a shot.
40. Bigger moves are tied to the sentence: follow a vehicle along its track (v1 s1, s4), orbit a container to reveal its
    other side (v1 s16), crane up a ramp to its top figure (v1 s12), follow a pen tip (v1 s11), pull back to make room for
    something that grows (v4 s2, s13), fly over a map and rise to top-down (v4 s9), dolly straight down the track for the
    whole last shot (v1 s19).
41. Camera height is LOW for vehicles and tall things (rail height, below the deck: v1 s1, s7; base of the coin stack: v4
    s4) and HIGH and slanted for maps and plans. Lenses feel wide: strong convergence, big foreground.
42. One camera move often carries two or three sentences and two or three object actions (v1 s3–4, s12, s16; v4 s1–2, s8):
    a "shot" is a small sequence.
43. Scene changes are HARD CUTS: 15 in v1 (6 round cards), 7 in v4 (4 round cards). In addition there are 2 whip pans in
    v1 and 4 in v4, each 6–10 frames of streak blur, used to jump between two things in the same thought (train → truck;
    stack → gauge → safe; map → coin).
44. There is NEVER a slow travel through empty darkness, a dissolve, a wipe, or a black frame between picture shots. The
    only black frames are the first 2–6 frames of a turn card and one 4-frame dip (v1, 8.6 s).
45. Picture shots last 0.8–13.4 s; the median is 4.2 s (v1) and 4.6 s (v4). Under 1 s only for a one-word punch ("fund.").

## G. Light
46. A soft key from above/front, strong pale rim light on edges, and a pool on the floor or a spot on the backdrop behind
    the subject. Shadows are soft; contact shadows are present under every object.
47. Light CHANGES as an event: station signs light as they are named (v1 s1); plaques light on their figure (v1 s2, s4,
    s16); the whole backdrop turns gold on "rich" (v4 s1); a red glow rises with a bad fact (v4 s8, s12); a glow blooms
    behind a card's word.
48. Bloom is only on emissive things: lit print, standing white numerals, coins in flight, glowing lines. Dark bodies and
    paper do not glow.
49. Coloured glows are large and soft (a third to a half of the frame), sit behind the subject, and are one colour per
    beat: gold for money made, green for growth, red for the bad fact.

## H. Turn cards
50. A card is a black frame with centred words and nothing else: no floor, no reflection, no object, no caption.
51. The lead-in is a small white regular grotesque, its words appearing one at a time on their spoken onsets (each on its
    own line when there are four or more: "But there / was one / problem.").
52. The key word is big, ITALIC and coloured, 290–450 px wide (40–63% of the frame width), with slight depth and a soft
    shadow: a bold italic sans in orange (v1) or a serif italic in gold or red (v4). It FLIPS in: for 2–3 frames it is seen
    squashed and blurred before it stands.
53. A radial glow in the word's colour blooms behind it over ~15 frames, ending about 560–700 px wide and 590–740 px tall,
    centred a little below the words (y ≈ 60%).
54. The words sit between 31% and 67% of the frame height. A card lasts 1.27–2.0 s. Hard cut in, hard cut out.
55. Card colour follows the turn: orange/gold for "Let us explain." and "But there was one problem."; red for "And here's
    the worst part."
56. Cards fall at about 17–21% of the runtime (after the hook), optionally near 45%, and at 76% (before the worst fact).

## I. Captions
57. One phrase of 1–4 words at a time, white, regular weight, neutral grotesque, about 22 px font at 720×1280 (1.75% of the
    frame height: 33–34 px at 1080×1920), centred on the frame's centre line, its vertical centre 14.7% down (y 188 of
    1280; y 282 of 1920), width 11–47% of the frame.
58. It swaps on the spoken phrase with a ~3-frame fade, never moves, never scales, has no highlight colour, no box and no
    second line. Numbers are written as figures ("from $4.7 billion").
59. Scene objects may pass behind it (the tag's rail, the map, a sign); it is not protected by a clear band.

## J. Script
60. 150–157 words a minute; 61–76 s; the voice starts on frame 0; the film ends 0.4–0.9 s after the last word.
61. Hook in three sentences and 10–15 s: (1) a plain setup naming the subject; (2) the number contrast; (3) what went wrong
    or what it costs the viewer. Then "Let us explain."
62. One fact per sentence, 3–24 words, mean 10–11. Dates lead sentences ("In 2010,", "In 2018,", "This year,").
63. Few figures (4–6 in a film), rounded, each said once or twice, hedged in one word ("about", "at least", "roughly",
    "up to", "more than").
64. The stakes are brought down to one person near the end ("about $500 from every Australian", "three cents in every
    dollar").
65. The ending returns to the OPENING image and sentence and lands a short punchline, often a fragment ("$14.5 billion
    later, it goes to Parkes." / "Australia let it sail away."), acted out by the opening's objects with one change.
66. Audience address: "your taxes", "we sell". No sources, rating, sign-off, logo or call to action are spoken or shown.
67. About 12 words per picture shot, but a new visual event every 1–2 s (an entrance, a light, a count, a drop).

## K. What they never do
68. No flat 2-D graphics, icons floating on the picture, lower thirds, progress bars, logos or watermarks (the only icons,
    v4 s8, are on solid tiles standing in the scene).
69. No documents to read: no newspaper pages, receipts, filings, certificates or screens of text.
70. No neon outline round objects, no glow on dark bodies, no white edge strips.
71. No photographs and no people.
72. No long empty travel, no fade to black between shots, no slow dissolves.
73. No more than one idea on screen: when a new figure arrives the old one has left or is out of frame.
74. No caption block, no word-by-word highlight, no text in the lower two thirds except what is on objects.
75. No pause in the voice longer than about 0.9 s (the cards).
